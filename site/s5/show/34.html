Shoes

Shoes is a cross-platform toolkit for writing graphical apps easily and artfully using Ruby.

Shoes includes:

  • Web-style layouts, styling and URLs
  • GUI elements: buttons, textfields, dropdowns, radios, etc
  • 2D drawing primitives (and animation!)
  • Mouse and keyboard polling / events

The Progenitor of Shoes

Shoes was originally conceived and coded by _why the lucky stiff, creator of the Poignant Guide to Ruby.

Shoes is now maintained by Team Shoes, a group of friendly Rubyist who can be found on the Shoes librelist or #shoes on freenode.

Stacks and Flows

Layout is handled using boxes called stacks and flows:

  • Elements within a stack, stack. (Like block level elements in HTML.)
  • Elements within a flow, flow. (Like inline elements in HTML.)

Let us silently judge these two apps:

   1  Shoes.app do                     Shoes.app do
   2    flow do                          stack do 
   3      button 'One'                     button 'One'
   4      button 'Two'                     button 'Two'
   5      button 'Buckle Shoe'             button 'Buckle Shoe'
   6    end                              end
   7  end                              end

Para Stands for Paragraph

Text is output with para:

   1  Shoes.app :title => 'Stacks' do
   2    stack do 
   3      para 'We are a way for the cosmos to know itself.'
   4      para 'Mutiple ', 'arguments ', 'are stitched ', 'together.'
   5    end
   6  end

Inline styles can be applied:

   1  Shoes.app :title => 'Q and A' do
   2    stack do
   3      para strong('Q. '), 'Do you believe in free will?'
   4      para em(strong('A. '), 'Nah, dream is destiny.')
   5    end
   6  end

Styling Text

Paragraphs can be styled.

Styles can be set using an options hash:

   1    para 'Lorizzle ipsum dolizzle sizzle amizzle.', :stroke => red

They can also be applied on a saved para object:

   1    @text = para 'Learn to Question. Question to Learn.'
   2    @text.stroke = blue
   3    @text.style(:font =>'Times 12px', :underline => 'single')

Your Users Have Fingers

Elements like edit_box and edit_line allow for user input:

   1  Shoes.app :height => 250, :width => 200 do
   2    stack :margin => 10 do
   3      @edit = edit_line :width => '90%' do 
   4        # Block triggered when content changes.
   5        @para.text = @edit.text
   6      end
   7      @para = para ''
   8      button 'what?' do
   9        alert 'I SAID: ' + @edit.text
  10      end
  11    end
  12  end

Images and You

Images are displayed using image.

Images can accept an optional click block and their source path can be changed at any time.

   1  Shoes.app :title => 'Soda Pups', :height => 375, :width => 610 do
   2    flow do
   3      image 'pup.png'
   4      @img = image 'pup.png' do
   5        @img.path = 'surprised_dog.jpg'
   6      end
   7    end
   8  end

2D Primitives

Drawing with Shoes is a snap.

   1  Shoes.app do
   2    fill rgb(0, 0.6, 0.9, 0.05)
   3    stroke rgb(0, 0.6, 0.9, 0.5)
   4  
   5    30.times do
   6      star(rand(self.width), rand(self.width), (5..7).rand)
   7    end
   8  
   9    fill rgb(0.6, 0, 0.8, 0.1)
  10    stroke rgb(0.6, 0, 0.8, 0.9)
  11  
  12    35.times do
  13      arrow(rand(self.width), rand(self.width), (20..100).rand)
  14    end
  15  end

Animation

Animation blocks re-draw at a target FPS.

   1  Shoes.app do
   2    trails = [[0,0]] * 60
   3    
   4    animate(24) do 
   5      clear do
   6        trails.shift
   7        trails << self.mouse[1,2]
   8        
   9        trails.each_with_index do |(x, y), i|
  10          oval :left => x, :top => y, :radius => i, :center => true
  11        end
  12      end
  13    end
  14  end

Shoes 4 and the Rainbow of Shoes

The Shoes Team has experimented with different implementations:

  • Red Shoes (Original Codebase GTK/Cocoa/C/Objective C/etc.)
  • White Shoes (RSpec test Suite)
  • Blue Shoes (Shoes on QT)
  • Green Shoes (Pure Ruby Shoes using GTK/Cairo)
  • Orange Shoes (Apps “Compile” to HTML5 Canvas JS)

Shoes 4.pre has just been released! See: Blog Announcement

Shoes 4 runs on JRuby. It’s a gem and a complete rewrite in 100% Ruby.
